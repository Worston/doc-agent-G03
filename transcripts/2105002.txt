What I learned about directing an AI assistant

The single most useful habit was refusing to accept a number without asking where
it came from. Three of the decisions in Section 4 look arbitrary on the form and
are not:

- **256 wordpieces, not 256 words.** The config said "chunk_tokens: 256" and the
  obvious reading is words. Measuring the corpus gave 1.32 wordpieces per word,
  so that reading would have produced ~338-piece windows and MiniLM would have
  silently truncated every one of them. Silently is the dangerous part — there is
  no error, just missing text at the end of every chunk.
- **numpy:flat, not the FAISS the template ships.** This started as a crash
  (`OMP: Error #15`, three copies of libomp) and I could have stopped at "faiss
  is broken on my Mac". The benchmark is what made it a defensible design choice
  instead of an excuse: at ~2,000 vectors exact search is faster *and* exact,
  and HNSW's approximation only pays off in the millions.
- **Redacting in place.** The obvious implementation returns a cleaned copy, and
  it would have scrubbed nothing, because the pipeline throws away the hook's
  return value and keeps using the original list. That is only visible by reading
  the caller.

Where I had to overrule the assistant, and where I let it run

I stopped the TrOCR retrain even though it was mid-flight, because the result
could not have changed which backend ships. I deferred both findings it raised at
the embedding stage — the mis-calibrated threshold and the hyphenation — because
neither belonged to the stage being built, and fixing the hyphenation would have
invalidated the measurements taken before it. I also held it to the smallest
possible PII implementation rather than letting it build a general detector.

Conversely, when it proposed measuring before writing — pricing the tokenizer
before choosing a window size, benchmarking three index backends before picking
one — I let it, and that is where the section's actual content came from.

What I would do differently

Two things. First, I would have caught the wordpiece/word distinction at design
time rather than at implementation time; it was in the encoder's documentation
the whole time. Second, I let the OCR fine-tuning run consume a large block of
time before checking on it, and the loss-shift bug meant two of those runs were
worthless. A cheap sanity check — decode 20 predictions and read them — would
have exposed "the model skips every other word" in minutes instead of hours.

Honest limits of what is claimed here

The fine-tuned TrOCR number quoted elsewhere in the project predates the
loss-shift fix and was never re-run, so it is an upper bound on that model's
error, not its score. The index numbers here come from a 40-page smoke build and
a 12-page verification, not the full 624-page corpus, which a teammate built
afterwards; the shapes and thresholds are the same but the counts differ. And the
`weak_threshold` in the config is, by my own measurement, wrong — it is recorded
as an open issue for A3 rather than quietly patched to make this milestone look
cleaner.
